Flight 15 ship return – what you're looking at

The path is a simulation, not live return telemetry. Coordinates come from the StarDash Flight 15 simulation (stardash.live), which StarDash describes as built from SpaceX's Flight 15 mission video and published timeline, the recorded entry of Flight 14, and physics. When this copy was checked (late morning CT, Sat Nov 14), Ship 42 was still in orbit; the return and catch had not happened yet.

What it's based on

  • Ground track, altitude, and speed – StarDash F15_SIM at 1 Hz. The published sim ends at Pad 2. This page keeps that shape and timing, then slides the last 75 seconds so the catch is the Pad 1 tower. The ship starts over the Pacific, crosses northern Mexico near 29–30°N and south Texas, passes north of South Padre and out over the Gulf, then turns south. stardash.live
  • Pad 1 tower – OpenStreetMap way 968227813, “Starbase Integration Tower 1” (OLIT-1 / Orbital Launch Pad 1), centroid 25.996126°N, 97.154736°W, structure height 143 m. Integration Tower 2 (way 1207227015) is 25.997046°N, 97.158067°W, about 100 m north and 330 m west, so Pad 1 is the eastern pad and slightly south of Pad 2. Catch altitude is the chopstick arms at 90 m, not the mast top. © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Approach description – SpaceX, "Returning Starship to the Launch Site" (Nov 7, 2026): reentry over the Pacific, over sparsely populated northern Mexico and southern Texas, north of South Padre Island and out over the Gulf, then a final turn south. The simulation follows that corridor. spacex.com/updates
  • Timeline – StarDash's Flight 15 event list matches SpaceX's published times: deorbit T+21:35:57–T+21:36:07, entry T+22:10:46, transonic T+22:29:34, subsonic T+22:30:13, landing burn T+22:32:16, flip T+22:32:18, 3→2 engines T+22:32:26, 2→1 T+22:32:33, catch T+22:32:34. Liftoff was 4:47:36 PM CT Nov 13 (confirmed by the live feed's T-0), so the default catch time is 3:20:10 PM CT, Sat Nov 14 (editable below). In the simulation the 100 km interface is T+22:06:37, and Mach 1 (ISA, ground speed) is about T+22:29:56 at 20.7 km, 22 seconds after the official transonic callout.
  • Map – Gulf/South Padre coastline and Rio Grande from OpenStreetMap (simplified); the Laguna Madre mainland shore is hand-approximated.
  • Rocket Ranch Outpost – approximate: placed on the Rio Grande 3.7 mi (Rocket Ranch's own straight-line figure) from the tower, ~25.958 N, 97.197 W. The campground (43742 Palmito Hill Rd) is ~25.947 N, 97.279 W.

Limits

  • This page is the phone camera view. On a computer it asks you to open it on your phone. The first tap anywhere starts the camera, motion, and location. On iPhone that tap is what allows motion. There is no AR button.
  • iPhone Safari has no WebXR AR, so on iOS this uses camera passthrough + compass (iOS 13+ asks for Motion permission). Android Chrome uses WebXR immersive-ar when supported.
  • Compass accuracy varies. On iPhone, heading is webkitCompassHeading (magnetic) plus declination. If accuracy is below 0 or worse than 20°, calibrate with a figure-8. Point the reticle at the real Pad 1 tower and tap Align to tower. The offset is saved on this phone.
  • Declination is a south-Texas constant of +2.5° E (true = magnetic + east declination), not a full World Magnetic Model run. Edit it if you are somewhere else. It is applied only to the magnetic compass, not to deviceorientationabsolute.
  • The portrait vertical field of view defaults to 63°, a typical iPhone 1× rear camera. Adjust it if the tower doesn't match the video.
  • Earth curvature is included in az/el; atmospheric refraction is not.
  • Cloud layers on the path come from an Open-Meteo forecast (low / mid / high cover, plus pressure-level cover when available) at the hour nearest catch. Grey path = likely hidden in or above >50% cloud; bright path = below those layers. This is a forecast, not live radar.
